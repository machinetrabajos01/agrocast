Metrics and why they were chosen

  • MAE (index points): average size of the error, easy to explain to users.
  • RMSE (index points): penalizes large misses, which are the costly ones for a purchase budget.
  • MASE: MAE divided by the one-month persistence error inside the training window. It has no units, can be averaged over horizons and origins, and compares directly with persistence. Main selection metric.
  • Diebold-Mariano test (with small-sample correction) at six months: checks whether the difference with persistence is statistically significant.
  • Interval coverage: share of actual values inside the 80% and 95% ranges.

MAPE was not used as the main metric because it is asymmetric; percentage errors are still shown in the bias analysis.

Cross-validation for time series

K-fold cross-validation shuffles months and lets a model learn from the future. AGROCAST uses rolling-origin evaluation with an expanding window: the model is fitted with data up to month t, forecasts t+1 … t+6, then the origin moves one month and everything is refitted.

  • ETS and SARIMA have an in-sample error about 1.5 times lower than their one-month validation error. The gap is moderate and expected for a series with a large shock; with 2 to 4 parameters there is little room for overfitting.
  • Ridge is the most flexible model and is the one that loses to persistence in both blocks: with 16 features and fewer than 80 rows, its extra flexibility does not generalize.
  • No underfitting signal: SARIMA residuals show no significant autocorrelation (Ljung-Box p = {{ ev.residual_diagnostics.ljung_box_p_lag6 }} at lag 6 and {{ ev.residual_diagnostics.ljung_box_p_lag12 }} at lag 12).

  • Positive error = forecast above the actual value. A model that follows the latest trend overestimates after a peak and underestimates during a fast rise; this is visible in 2021-2022 and in April-May 2026.
  • Risks: a new international shock, a change of the UPRA methodology or basket, and the short test block (12 origins), which is too small to generalize.
  • The model forecasts an index, not the price a producer pays; the product price in each plan comes from SIPSA-I.
