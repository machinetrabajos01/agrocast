Project documentation (PGC)

Forecasting Colombian agricultural input price variations with CRISP-ML(Q)

Machine Learning · Systems Engineering · University of Cundinamarca, Chía extension. This page summarizes the approved project; the full methodological documentation is available in the Project and Models menus.

01 · Problem

Why it matters

Fertilizers and pesticides are a major share of production costs. When their prices rise in ways that are hard to anticipate, producers face uncertainty when they prepare budgets and schedule purchases. UPRA publishes a monthly national index of agricultural input prices (January 2021 = 100) built from DANE SIPSA-I retail prices.

Guiding question: what performance do statistical and machine learning models, organized with CRISP-ML(Q), achieve when forecasting the national index six months ahead compared with simple reference models?

02 · Objectives

General objective

Develop and evaluate a CRISP-ML(Q) prototype that forecasts the variation of the national agricultural input price index six months ahead from official historical series, as reference information for cost planning.

  • Characterize user needs, success criteria, scope and risks.
  • Collect and audit UPRA and SIPSA-I series.
  • Prepare a reproducible monthly series without future information.
  • Train and compare reference and forecasting models.
  • Evaluate out of sample with rolling origins and uncertainty.
  • Deliver a decision-support system with documented limitations.

03 · Data & method

Sources and evaluation

UPRA national index (total, fertilizers, pesticides), DANE SIPSA-I prices by department and product, Banco de la República exchange rate and World Bank fertilizer prices. Candidates (ETS, SARIMA, Ridge with external variables) were compared with persistence and seasonal naive baselines using rolling-origin validation and a separate final test block.

04 · Information system

From forecast to decision

Producers register farms and purchase plans priced with SIPSA-I. The server applies the SARIMA model to the official index stored in the database and recommends buying now, deferring or buying as planned, with the projected cost and an 80% range. Analysts maintain the index records and monitor the model; administrators manage users, roles and model versions.

System design Technical documentation
Limitations. The index is a national average and does not represent the price of every shop, region or presentation. SARIMA beat persistence in validation but not in the final test block, so recommendations support planning and do not replace supplier quotes.
